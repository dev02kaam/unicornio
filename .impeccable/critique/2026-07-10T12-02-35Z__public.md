## Anti-pattern verdict

The interface is coherent but reads as a generated educational template: repeated identical cards, a badge over almost every section, excessive lavender, 30 px rounding, wide shadows, floating decoration and automatic sparkle icons. That tone is too playful and visually noisy for consent and future sensitive screening workflows.

The bundled detector returned one `overused-font` warning for `Arial`; it is a false positive because the declaration is a system-font fallback permitted by the product register. Actionable detector findings: **0**.

## Priority issues

### P1

1. Modals open without moving focus, trapping Tab, restoring focus or making the background inert.
2. No skip link exists on any page.
3. Dynamic API/user values are inserted with `innerHTML`, enabling persistent HTML injection into administrative views.
4. Login lacks autocomplete metadata, an announced error and double-submit prevention.
5. The tablet dashboard grid stretches short family content into very tall empty cards.
6. Quick navigation hides every text label and communicates it only through hover/focus tooltips.

### P2

1. The student dashboard can render the literal word `undefined` because a relationship note is absent.
2. The legal summary can remain stuck on “Cargando versión legal...” after data loads.
3. The student page keeps the document title “Mi hijo/a”.
4. Tables have no captions and generated column headers have no `scope`.
5. Decorative motion and layered shadow/gradient treatments compete with sensitive content.
6. Profile “preference” toggles look functional but do not persist or affect behavior.

## Persona red flags

- **Sam, keyboard or screen-reader user:** cannot bypass navigation and can tab behind a modal; async feedback may not be announced.
- **Jordan, first-time user:** icon-only navigation, false preference controls and stale loading labels undermine confidence.
- **Casey, family on a portrait tablet:** the dashboard creates large blank regions and does not clearly protect accidental consent decisions.

## Minor observations

- Base token contrast is acceptable: body text, muted text and primary buttons meet AA in measured combinations.
- Sampled controls other than the login back link meet a 44 px target.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` exists and sampled layouts did not overflow horizontally.
- The custom select and pointer-only column drag need a dedicated assistive-technology review.

## Questions to consider

- Should non-persistent profile preferences be removed until there is a real contract?
- Which consent operations legally require confirmation, reason capture or a second factor?
- Which identity fields are truly necessary for each role to see?
